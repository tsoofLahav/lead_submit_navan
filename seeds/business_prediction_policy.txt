Business Prediction Policy
Demo edition | 19 November 2026

1. Business Framework

1.1. Purpose and decision paths
The system recommends the appropriate level of sales and account support. It distinguishes three decisions:
- Initial lead assignment: deterministic classification from submitted annual GBV and employee count.
- Lead evaluation: forecast the most suitable support model from public research and submitted context. This is a prediction, not proof of future usage or quantitative eligibility.
- Customer re-evaluation: assess current tier suitability using reported Navan activity; use quantitative tier criteria as decision guidance, not approval prerequisites.

The AI interprets evidence and recommends business actions. The backend owns calculations, classification, structural validation and approval. No recommendation automatically changes a tier.

1.2. Tier framework
Tier | Quantitative criteria (initial assignment and customer assessment) | Support model
Tier 1 | Annual GBV >= $500,000 | Dedicated AE and AM
Tier 2 | Annual GBV >= $100,000 or employees >= 150, unless Tier 1 applies | AE-led adoption and launch
Tier 3 | All other companies for initial assignment; lower spend and headcount indicate Tier 3 for customer assessment | Primarily product-led

Initial assignment requires a submitted annual GBV estimate; employee count is optional. The estimate may overstate or understate potential. Missing customer measurements never establish that a threshold is met or not met.

2. Evidence and Uncertainty

2.1. Evidence quality
Assess reliability for the specific claim: official company reports and observed product data generally have high reliability; company announcements and established publications medium to high; third-party databases medium; unverified sources low. Consider relevance, specificity, scope, recency and consistency. Demo activity and submitted lead values are user-reported, not independently verified facts. Distinguish source reliability from confidence in an inference.

Direct evidence explicitly reports a fact, estimate or range. Indirect evidence supports a reasoned inference from genuine context. Both are valid for forecasts; neither establishes an unobserved outcome as fact. Never invent values, sources, dates or scope.

2.2. Missing and conflicting information
Missing information is unknown, not zero, low potential or evidence against a recommendation. Limit its effect to dependent conclusions. Retain independently supported findings.
Compare conflicting observations by entity, population, measurement and period before treating them as contradictions. Prefer the most credible relevant observation, not automatically the newest. If plausible alternatives yield the same classification, retain it. If a supported interpretation is preferable, use it and reflect material uncertainty in confidence. Otherwise preserve the conflict and constrain affected conclusions. Count duplicate underlying observations once.
For leads, ordinary uncertainty about future usage, rollout, travel frequency or GBV belongs in confidence and material follow-up, not an eligibility gate. For customers, unresolved uncertainty about a threshold measurement limits the strength of the recommendation, not the human’s authority to approve it.

3. Lead Evaluation: Forecast-Based Suitability

3.1. Targeted research
Evaluate the submitted company as a whole unless the submission or credible evidence identifies a narrower entity or rollout. Unknown rollout scope is not evidence of a limited rollout. Do not invent a hypothetical narrower scope to veto a supported forecast. Respect actual evidence of narrower scope and avoid attributing unrelated populations to it.

Signal | Assessment basis | Priority
Company size | Employee count, estimate or range | High
External business activity | Client-facing sales, consulting, partnerships and deployments | High
Geographic distribution | Offices, international operations and distributed teams | High
Corporate events | Conferences and employee gatherings | Optional
Business growth & expansion | Revenue, headcount, market or office expansion | Medium
Operational complexity | Organizational structure and coordination requirements | Medium

Aim for two or three focused searches overall within runtime limits. Verify identity; combine size, external activity and geography where practical, reuse evidence for growth and complexity. Use concise authoritative excerpts or attributable snippets; open sources only when necessary. Hiring, funding, industry and work model are contextual, not separate search targets. Search separately for events only if material.
Seek additional evidence only if it could materially change a prediction or useful decision. Do not search for proof of future Navan GBV, actual trip counts or rollout plans as prerequisites. Stop when evidence is sufficient for useful forecasts or further retrieval is unlikely to improve them within budget. Use the smallest adequate search context; pass compact structured findings, not raw pages, downstream.

Represent all six signals exactly once with value or range, relevant source URLs and verified full dates when available, reliability (High/Medium/Low), evidence type (Direct/Indirect), status (Found/Estimated/Conflicting/Missing), and material gaps. Default to one or two strong observations per signal. Missing has no evidence items and an explicit gap. Preserve distinct observations and provenance; never force coverage.

3.2. Travel spend potential
Estimate qualitative potential from company scale and expected travel intensity. Do not produce a numeric GBV forecast.

Employees | Scale
1-49 | Small
50-149 | Medium
150-499 | Large
500+ | Very Large

The backend derives scale from applicable counts, estimates, inclusive ranges or one-sided bounds. Use preferred supported evidence; if unresolved plausible values fit one band, retain it. If meaningful uncertainty crosses bands without a supported preference, or the evidence cannot be attributed to the evaluated entity, scale is Unknown.

Intensity signal | Weight
External business activity | 2
Geographic distribution | 2
Corporate events | 1

Rate each Low (0), Medium (1) or High (2), relative to company scale. Credible qualitative evidence can support the most plausible rating without direct trip measurements. Explain the connection to expected travel; distributed offices or partnerships alone do not automatically imply High. Admit relevant medium/high-reliability direct or indirect evidence supporting a resolved rating.
The backend calculates weighted mean = sum(weight * rating) / sum(admitted weights), with coverage = admitted weight / 5. Exclude missing ratings from the mean. Use unrounded scores: below 0.67 Low; 0.67-1.33 Medium; above 1.33 High.
Require supported scale and at least one core signal (external activity or geography). One core caps confidence at Medium; High confidence requires both core signals with reliable consistent evidence and no material unresolved uncertainty. Events alone are insufficient. Coverage informs confidence, not a completeness gate.

Intensity / Scale | Small | Medium | Large | Very Large
Low | Low | Low | Medium | High
Medium | Low | Medium | High | High
High | Medium | High | High | Very High

3.3. Growth potential
Primary evidence: business growth and expansion; external activity and geography provide context.
High: strong growth or expansion likely to increase travel demand.
Medium: moderate growth or multiple consistent indirect indicators.
Low: evidence of stable or declining travel demand.
Unknown: insufficient relevant evidence.
Require one relevant medium/high-reliability primary observation or two independent medium/high-reliability secondary observations pointing in the same direction over comparable periods. Hiring or funding alone does not establish High. Explain the expected relationship to travel, without requiring proof of actual trips.

3.4. Expected support needs
Primary evidence: operational complexity; size and geography are supporting signals; growth is contextual.
High: significant organizational or geographic complexity suggesting substantial onboarding or ongoing coordination.
Medium: moderate complexity suggesting targeted assistance.
Low: evidence of relatively simple operations.
Unknown: insufficient evidence.
Require relevant medium/high-reliability evidence of complexity, including supported inference from scale, distributed operations or structure. Predict expected assistance, not confirmed implementation requirements.

3.5. Forecast confidence
Assess each prediction independently using relevant evidence, reliability, coverage, consistency and inference strength.
High: strong, consistent evidence for the important signals, with no material unresolved uncertainty.
Medium: sufficient evidence with ordinary forecast uncertainty or meaningful but non-disqualifying gaps.
Low: minimum evidence met but inference is weak or substantially uncertain.
Insufficient: minimum evidence not met; return Unknown for that prediction.
Indirect evidence is not automatically low confidence. Missing secondary signals do not invalidate supported primary conclusions. Each prediction includes level, confidence, supporting signals, rationale and material gaps.

3.6. Lead recommendation
Choose the most plausible support model for the evaluated company from the available evidence. Travel potential is the primary indicator; growth informs future opportunity and support needs inform human assistance.

Travel potential | Baseline suitable tiers | Alignment for current tiers 3 / 2 / 1
Low | Tier 3 | Consistent / Potential overclassification / Potential overclassification
Medium | Tier 2 | Potential underclassification / Consistent / Potential overclassification
High | Tier 2 or 1 | Potential underclassification / Consistent / Consistent
Very High | Tier 1 | Potential underclassification / Potential underclassification / Consistent
Unknown | No travel-based target | Undetermined / Undetermined / Undetermined

Calculate alignment deterministically. Choose Tier 1 for High travel when evidence supports dedicated assistance or opportunity; otherwise Tier 2 is the normal baseline. Growth and support may justify departure from a baseline with explicit evidence-backed business reasoning. Low support alone does not establish low travel. Reliable researched headcount may independently justify correcting initial underclassification. If travel is Unknown, other supported evidence may establish a specific support-model fit; otherwise leave the target unresolved.

Use submitted GBV and headcount as context, not as numerical eligibility constraints on forecast recommendations. Explain substantial discrepancies without overwriting submitted values. Do not require evidence of future usage, actual onboarding scope, a public travel budget or proof that dedicated management will in fact be necessary. For instance, supported Very High travel and High support forecasts at Medium confidence normally justify Tier 1 even with $50,000 submitted GBV and unknown rollout scope. A real contradictory fact may alter that conclusion; the mere possibility of a narrower rollout does not.

Assess recommendation confidence from the evidence driving the selected tier, not the weakest prediction. Return the most plausible supported tier even at Low confidence. Retain only if the current tier is supported. Use Undetermined only when no meaningful evidence supports a specific preference. All supported Upgrade and Downgrade recommendations are available for human approval, irrespective of confidence. Do not claim numerical eligibility for forecast-based recommendations.

4. Customer Re-evaluation: Observed Activity

4.1. Inputs and derived metrics
Use only the latest validated activity snapshot and explicitly reported current tier. Demo snapshots are manually entered summaries since signup, not monthly records. Do not use earlier lead research, forecasts, submitted annual GBV or external web research.
- months_since_signup: completed months, required positive integer.
- total_gbv: total USD GBV since signup.
- avg_monthly_bookings: average monthly bookings since signup.
- employee_count: current positive employee count.
- avg_monthly_active_users and avg_monthly_support_requests: monthly averages since signup.
- travel_spend_trend, company_size_trend, product_adoption_trend: increasing/stable/decreasing/unknown, user-reported.
All activity measurements except months may be missing; zero is valid for GBV, bookings, users and requests. Trends are qualitative, not verified growth rates.
Calculate deterministically with known inputs and positive denominators:
Annualized GBV = total_gbv / months_since_signup * 12 (indicative run rate; stronger evidence after three completed months).
Estimated adoption (%) = 100 * avg_monthly_active_users / employee_count (historical mean divided by current count; values above 100% may reflect differing time bases).
Monthly support rate = 100 * avg_monthly_support_requests / avg_monthly_active_users.
Unavailable or inconsistent inputs produce unavailable metrics as applicable; do not invent values.

4.2. When to evaluate
Evaluate the first valid snapshot and every change in normalized effective inputs or reported current tier, including known-to-unknown and newly known values. Skip equivalent submissions only when already successfully evaluated under the current policy. Failed/interrupted work is retryable; prevent duplicate concurrent work. Prior snapshots serve change detection only. Store snapshot history; compare business values, not formatting, IDs or timestamps.
Compute contextual indicators: possible eligibility mismatch; any increasing/decreasing reported trend; support rate >= 5 per 100 active users. Unknown inputs yield unknown indicators. Indicators are not initiation gates. Sparse new submissions still receive an assessment. For equivalent completed submissions, report unchanged activity, not a new Retain recommendation.

4.3. Customer recommendation
Assess business suitability from observed Navan activity using available measurements, duration, bookings, adoption, support and reported trends. Apply the quantitative criteria in Section 1.2 as the primary customer decision framework. Missing optional inputs affect only dependent conclusions; do not require a complete form, infer low headcount from missing headcount or treat unknown trends as stable.

Known annualized GBV >= $500,000 supports Tier 1. GBV >= $100,000 or employee count >= 150 supports Tier 2 unless Tier 1 is better supported. GBV below $500,000 with evidence supporting Tier 2 favors a downgrade from Tier 1. GBV below $100,000 and employee count below 150 strongly support Tier 3. When a criterion is unknown, report that uncertainty rather than treating it as met or unmet. A low observed run rate may support Tier 3 despite unknown headcount; substantial activity or support needs may support an upgrade despite incomplete measurements. Explain material departures from known quantitative criteria, including contrary evidence. Short observation duration reduces confidence; three completed months provide a stronger basis, not a mandatory approval condition. Adoption is contextual, never a downgrade prerequisite.

Use trends, bookings, adoption and support to explain business implications. Increasing spend, size or adoption may indicate expansion; growing size with flat/declining adoption or declining spend/adoption may indicate barriers; high support rate may justify assistance. Average bookings measure volume, not growth. Trends alone do not establish quantitative thresholds or verified persistence.

Recommend the best-supported tier, including a change at Low confidence where justified. Retain requires affirmative support; Undetermined is reserved for absence of a meaningful preference. Quantitative criteria guide the recommendation, not approval. The human may accept or reject any supported change after reviewing its confidence and limitations.

4.4. Customer confidence
High: not available from demo summary snapshots alone, because monthly stability cannot be verified.
Medium: at least three completed months and coherent measurements sufficiently supporting the business conclusion, even if a separate eligibility condition remains unverified.
Low: short duration, sparse evidence or meaningful unresolved uncertainty about business suitability despite some support.
Insufficient: no meaningful evidence for a business preference.
Low or zero activity volume alone does not lower confidence when coherent measurements support the conclusion. Missing an optional threshold input does not automatically imply Insufficient confidence. Low confidence permits a supported recommendation for human approval; Insufficient indicates that no meaningful preference can be established.

5. Recommendation and Presentation

Use existing structured fields and source references. Recommendation direction is Retain, Upgrade, Downgrade or Undetermined; recommended_tier is current tier for Retain, supported target for Upgrade/Downgrade, null for Undetermined. Every supported Upgrade/Downgrade is pending human approval, regardless of confidence, incomplete measurements or unverified quantitative criteria. State material uncertainty in the rationale, without inventing a separate conditional status. Preserve confidence, findings, rationale, follow-ups and information gaps. Alignment, recommendation and approval are separate concepts.
Default display: recommendation, confidence and decision status first, then three lead predictions or relevant customer assessment, then material next steps. Keep detailed evidence, metrics and sources available on expansion; do not show internal execution diagnostics as business findings.
Rationale normally <= 50 words; prediction rationale <= 30 words each; research description <= 40 words per signal excluding metadata. State the strongest reason and material limitation; avoid repeating facts across sections. Default to at most two distinct useful next steps in total, combining gaps with verification actions. Do not request information merely because it is absent. Omit empty or duplicate sections. Chat answers are concise by default. Length targets are not hard validation gates; retain material caveats and provenance.

6. Validation and Approval

Hard validation enforces schema, workspace isolation, provenance, valid target/direction, input integrity and consistency with supported evidence. Do not suppress a supported recommendation or block its approval because confidence is Low, quantitative criteria are unmet or unverified, observation duration is short, or optional information is missing. Quantitative criteria remain deterministic for initial assignment and inform customer assessment; they are not human approval gates. Soft validation preserves warnings and gaps for incomplete, weak, outdated or conflicting evidence without terminating usable evaluations.
Compute derived metrics, scale and alignment deterministically. Normalize safe formatting and duplicate references; isolate defective individual observations when independent evidence can be preserved, update affected statuses and conclusions, and never silently alter facts. If a recoverable defect cannot be corrected safely in code, allow one bounded tool-free correction attempt per stage with original context, invalid output and precise diagnostics. Never repeat completed research for correction. If correction fails and safe isolation is impossible, stop the affected stage and preserve completed work.

Only explicit human acceptance of a supported pending Upgrade/Downgrade changes the operational tier; record tier history and verify freshness. Retain and Undetermined do not change it. Approval checks enforce authorization, freshness, valid pending status and target integrity, not business confidence or quantitative thresholds. Material subsequent updates supersede outdated pending recommendations. The explicitly reported customer tier is authoritative external information; record an external change separately from recommendation approval. Preserve snapshot and workspace boundaries.

All numerical thresholds beyond the stated tier framework, estimation weights, matrices and decision heuristics are initial demo assumptions subject to calibration using historical Navan customer data. Runtime budgets must be enforced by configuration and monitored; policy wording alone does not bound tool-returned tokens.
